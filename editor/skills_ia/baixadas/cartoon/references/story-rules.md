# Story rules

## The shape (works for 40 seconds or 6 minutes)
1. **Once upon a time / where.** One line that paints the world: "At the edge of the sea stood a lighthouse."
2. **Who + what they want.** One small hero with one clear want: "a little fox named Pip dreamed of touching the moon."
3. **They try.** One concrete action kids can see: building, climbing, swimming.
4. **The problem.** Gentle and small. The light goes out; the hug hurts; the lamps point the wrong way.
5. **Friends help.** Kindness solves it, not luck. Each friend brings one thing.
6. **The warm ending line.** Short, quotable, a little wise: "Dreams are made to fly." / "The right hug always finds you."

## Language
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence, so each sentence becomes a scene.
- Sound words kids repeat: *bump!*, *OUCH!*, *whoosh*, "Three… two… one…".
- Name the hero early and use the name again.
- Repetition with a twist ("…each with a little light", "…each showing her the way").
- Bedtime stories end slower and quieter than they start. Bright stories end on a cheer.

## Length guide (narration words)
| Film | Bright / bouncy | Calm bedtime |
|---|---|---|
| 1 minute | ~100 words | ~65 words |
| 5 minutes | ~500 words | ~330 words |
| 10 minutes | ~1,000 words | ~650 words |

## Characters
- One look per character, written down once (colour, shape, one accessory: Pip's goggles, Prickles' red scarf) and kept the same in every scene and every episode.
- Animals with simple silhouettes read best in felt: fox, mouse, hedgehog, whale, turtle, rabbit, owl.
- Give each friend a "light" or a "gift" they bring at the climax.

## Ages
- **2–4:** no peril, lots of repetition, bright colours, 1–2 characters.
- **4–6:** a small problem, 3–4 friends, a funny moment.
- **6–8:** a real choice, a twist, a slightly bigger world.

## Series ideas that keep each episode different
Same hero, new place every episode (the moon, the deep sea, the snowy forest). Or the same world with a new hero each time. Or one value per episode (sharing, courage, patience). Change the problem and the ending every time, not just the names.
