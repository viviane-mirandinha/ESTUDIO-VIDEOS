In 1959, nine experienced hikers vanished in the Ural Mountains of Soviet Russia. Weeks later, their tent was found slashed open from the inside. The hikers had fled into minus forty temperatures wearing almost nothing. Some had fractured skulls. One was missing her tongue. Soviet investigators ruled it an unknown compelling force. The case became known as the Dyatlov Pass incident. For sixty years, theories ranged from military tests to alien encounters to a yeti attack. But in 2020, Swiss researchers finally offered a scientific explanation. Using advanced avalanche simulations, they showed that a small slab avalanche, triggered by accumulated snow on a cut slope above the tent, could have struck with enough force to cause the exact injuries found on the bodies. The hikers woke in darkness, panicked, cut their way out, and fled downhill. In the freezing night, hypothermia did the rest. The mystery that haunted Russia for decades may have been solved by snow, gravity, and human terror.
